What a Strong Proposal Contains

While every proposal is tailored, most successful documents share a common structure that funders expect to see.

Core Sections We Typically Include

  • Cover page and summary – so a reviewer can quickly understand the request
  • Background and problem statement – why the project is needed
  • Project objectives and expected outcomes – what success looks like
  • Description of activities – what will actually be done
  • Implementation plan and timeline – how and when work will happen
  • Beneficiaries and location – who will benefit and where
  • Budget and justification – clear costs linked to activities
  • Management and sustainability – how the project will be run and continue after funding
  • Monitoring and reporting – how progress will be tracked
  • Organisational capacity – why your group is able to deliver

How We Work With You

We do not invent projects. We take the real situation, goals and capacity of your group or business and present them in a professional format that funders can assess. The quality of the final proposal depends heavily on the information you provide.

During the process we will ask for details such as:

  • Current situation and the specific problem you want to address
  • What you have already done or contributed
  • Exact location and number of people who will benefit
  • Realistic activities and estimated costs
  • Who will manage the project day-to-day
  • Any existing partnerships or support

The more accurate and complete the information, the stronger and more credible the proposal becomes.

Important Notes About Funding Proposals

Writing a strong proposal significantly improves your chances, but it does not guarantee funding. Final decisions rest with the funding body and depend on available budgets, priorities, competition and due diligence. Our role is to present your project clearly, honestly and professionally so it can be properly considered.

We encourage clients to be realistic about budgets, timelines and their own capacity to implement. Overly ambitious claims can damage credibility. A well-scoped, achievable project is usually more persuasive than an exaggerated one.

If you already have a draft or notes, we can also help strengthen and restructure existing material rather than starting from zero.
